Zara is standing outside her ex's wedding, in a black suit, holding a box of buttonholes.

Thirty days ago she said yes.

Today is the day.

It rained.

She had umbrellas.

His gran could hear the band.

The bride got her ten quiet minutes.

Then line fourteen.

She pinned the flower.

He said, thanks for doing this.

She said, of course.

Nine seconds. She counted.

She found that she did.

Not all the way. Enough.

She cued the first dance, and stepped back.

Nobody looked at her.

It was the best day's work of her life.

At table nine there was one chair, and it was hers.

Later, in the car park, she opens the app.

She writes: I was there.

It says: you were there, all of you, the whole day.

Then one question. Where to now?

One last vote.

Does she dance, or does she go home?

What would you do?

Zero days.
