# What makes a Heartly video work

From Odi's saved examples (TikTok collections "Good ad example" and "Bad ad example, why?"), studied 15 Nov 2026. Read this before writing any script.

**The goal right now:** make people react. They should feel something, comment, save, share, and come away feeling they'd be silly not to act. This doesn't mean being robotic or salesy.

## The examples

### Good: "The person in the middle" (@jermy_islockd)
- **Numbers:** 41 s, 13.3K likes, 50 comments, 4.5K saves.
- **Look and sound:** white line icons on black. Music only, no voiceover. The only text is labels of 1–2 words: SUPPLY, DISTRIBUTOR +10%, DEMAND, MAKE, MATCH, you.
- **Structure:**
  1. A one-sentence idea in the caption: "The person in the middle never makes the product, and still gets paid on every order."
  2. A visual metaphor you get in 2 seconds (farm, middle person, shop).
  3. The money moving.
  4. The same pattern across 4 industries.
  5. It ends with "you" standing in the middle.
- **Why it works:**
  - A useful insight most people haven't put into words.
  - No jargon, and it escalates.
  - It ends by putting the viewer in the picture, so they start thinking "how do I do that?"
  - Saves run at 1 for every 3 likes.
  - The product never appears. The top comment is "The reason I love this app".

### Good: "50 uncomfortable questions, Day 1/50" (@freeyourmiind_, photo post)
- **Numbers:** 423K likes, 15.4K comments, 52.3K saves.
- **Content:** a cover, then one question: "If you walked into a room with everyone you've ever known, who would be the first person you look for?"
- **Why it works:**
  - Everyone answers privately, then publicly in the comments: dead parents, exes, their old self.
  - Almost no production.
  - The series promise (day 1 of 50) gives a reason to follow.

### Bad: "Lighter without noticing" (@gymaesthetic03, an ad for GymLog AI)
- **Numbers:** 47 s, 10 likes, 0 comments, 3 saves.
- **What it does:** it copies the middleman look, but:
  - The hook is vague jargon ("when you train / lighter without noticing / you think vs you lift").
  - It cites a 2004 journal paper and has lots of small text to read.
  - The insight is small: you pick lighter weights than recommended.
  - The fix is obvious and free: write your numbers down.
  - About 40% of the runtime is the app: logo, screenshots, "ask the AI coach", App Store badge.
- **The result:** the viewer feels sold to, not helped. It is less useful, less clear and less about the viewer.

## Rules
1. **Payoff first.** Open with something useful, surprising, or that names exactly what the viewer feels. It should be understood in 2 seconds, with no jargon.
2. **One idea.** Show it as a visual metaphor rather than text. Labels are 1–3 words. Narration, if used, is plain speech.
3. **Escalate.** Go from one example, to it being everywhere, to it being you.
4. **End on the viewer, with the gap still open.**
   - They should already be thinking "how do I do that / fix that?"
   - Heartly can be the next step, in one line, by what it does ("so she opened Heartly and wrote out what was really going on").
   - Never a logo, App Store badge or feature tour.
5. **Make it answerable.** Leave a question or prompt people want to answer in the comments.
6. **Be honest.**
   - No made-up stats, fake studies or clinical claims.
   - If you cite something, it must be well known and stated carefully.
7. **The edit matters.** Use one consistent style, smooth motion, readable type and generous spacing. Avoid cluttered screens.

## Formats to test
- **(a) Mechanism explainer** (middleman style): "why your brain keeps doing X", line art, ending with "you".
- **(b) Story** (the current stick-figure stories): setup, struggle, turn, release.
- **(c) One-question post:** a single uncomfortable question as a series ("day 1/30").

## Script rules (from the skills in CLAUDE.md)
- **Hook:** the first line must score 70+ on `tools/ig-reel/hookscore.py`. Open on the best scene, with motion and the hook text on frame 1. No greeting or set-up.
- **Shape:** hook (0–2 s), why it matters (2–7 s), one idea per beat with the picture changing every beat, payoff, then one ask.
- **On-screen text** is its own script: six words or fewer per card.
- **One ask at the end.** Make it a real question people can answer, and echo a word from the hook.
- **No AI tells.** Never write:
  - "It's not X, it's Y" or "it was never about X" contrast reveals. Say the point directly.
  - "No X, no Y, no Z" lists.
  - A claim followed by a comma and more clauses restating it.
  - Self-answered questions and colon reveals ("The result? …").
  - Stock phrases ("here's the thing", "say goodbye to", "take it to the next level").
  - Em dashes.
  - Engagement bait ("thoughts?", "read that again") and manufactured vulnerability.
- **Never invent** numbers, studies or results.
