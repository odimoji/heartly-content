### no_contact (format a, mechanism explainer; angle: correcting)
- Hook (on screen and spoken, frame 0): "60 days of no contact and you still think about them every day. here's why."
- Teach: no contact stops new contact. It doesn't touch the loop in your head. The loop runs on unanswered questions (why, what if, was it me), and your brain keeps replaying them because it treats them as unfinished business.
- Visual idea: a door being shut on a figure labelled "them" (no contact), while inside the character's head three question marks keep circling. Shutting the door harder does nothing to the circle. Then each question is pulled out onto a page and stops moving.
- Do: take the questions out of your head and answer them one at a time, in writing.
- Turn (narrator, once): "so he opened Heartly, wrote the first one down, and it showed him the question underneath."
- End on the viewer: "which question is yours?" (answerable in comments)
- Caption: no contact was never the hard part. (the app is heartly) #nocontact #breakup
- Honesty: no stats, no study names. "unfinished business" is plain speech, not a clinical claim.

### worse_at_night (format a, mechanism explainer; angle: validating)
- Hook: "fine all day. wrecked at 2am. you're not going backwards."
- Teach: in the day your attention is taken. At night nothing competes with the loop, and being tired makes everything feel more final than it is.
- Visual idea: daytime, the thought is a small thing in a crowded room of other things (work, bus, friends, phone). Night, the room empties one item at a time until the thought is the only thing left and fills the room. Same size thought, emptier room.
- Do: don't argue with it in your head at 2am. Get it out of your head and onto a page.
- Turn (narrator, once): "so she opened Heartly and wrote out what was really going on."
- End on the viewer: "what's yours at 2am?"
- Caption: it's not bigger at night. the room is just emptier. (the app is heartly) #overthinking #2am
- Honesty: no stats or clinical terms.
